Government Procurement Hub (GPH) helps manufacturers and distributors find federal contract opportunities by indexing solicitations from DLA, DIBBS, and SAM.gov and matching them to your product catalog.

When you sign in, your dashboard shows a **Get started** checklist with three steps. Completing all three activates your dashboard and starts your solicitation alerts.

## Step 1: Search a part

The dashboard's **Search by NSN/NIIN** bar is the fastest way to look up a part you make or sell. For more options, open **Library Search** in the navigation — you can search by NSN/NIIN, Solicitation number, Mfg Part Number, Contract number, or Description.

A part record opens with detailed information across eight tabs, including procurement history, recent solicitations, manufacturers, and technical specifications.

→ Full guide: [Searching for Parts](/help/parts-search)

## Step 2: Look up a vendor

Use the dashboard's **Look up a vendor by CAGE** bar to research any government contractor by their CAGE code. To search by company name or UEI instead, open the full **Library Search**.

A vendor profile shows business details, SAM registration, contacts, recent awards, and a 13-month contract history broken down by DLA distribution center.

Look up your own CAGE to see how GPH represents your business, or research competitors to understand who's winning awards on parts you supply.

→ Full guide: [Researching Vendors](/help/vendor-research)

## Step 3: Set up a bid-matching profile

Bid-matching profiles tell GPH which solicitations to monitor for you. Once a profile is active, matches appear daily under the **Bid-Matching** tab in the navigation, and email alerts are sent based on your plan and notification preferences.

To create your first profile, go to **Account \> Bid-Matching Profile** and click **Create Profile**.

*Note: profile limits vary by plan — Free includes 1 profile, Basic includes 5, and Advanced includes 20\.*

→ How to create one: [Setting Up Bid-Matching Profiles](/help/bid-matching-profiles) → How matching works: [How Solicitation Matching Works](/help/solicitation-matching)
