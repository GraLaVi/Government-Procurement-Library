**Procurement Analytics** turns the data behind your searches into a single view of your market and your position in it: what the government is buying right now, where you've won before, who you're up against, and which opportunities are worth your time this week. You'll find it under **Analytics** in the top navigation.

Procurement Analytics is an add-on on the Advanced plan, and it's assigned per user (a seat). If you don't hold a seat, the Analytics page shows an upgrade prompt instead of the dashboard — see [Plans and pricing](/help/plans-and-pricing#add-ons). It also adds the [Demand & Stock tab](/help/demand-intelligence) to every part record.

Everything scoped to "your business" is keyed to your account's **CAGE code**. If your CAGE code isn't set, those sections can't be built — contact support to have it added.

## Market Pulse

The top section is the whole market, identical for every customer — the backdrop against which your own numbers make sense.

- **DIBBS Open Solicitations** and **SAM.gov DoD Open Solicitations** — how many opportunities are live right now on each source.
- **Recent DIBBS Awards (90d)** — total award value over the last 90 days, a rough measure of how much money is actually moving.
- **Set-aside market** — how the market splits across small-business, HUBZone, SDVOSB, and other set-aside designations. If you hold a designation, this is the slice of the market you can compete in with less competition.
- **Opportunity trend** — SAM.gov opportunity volume over time, so you can tell a quiet week from a structural slowdown.

Use this section to sanity-check the rest: a drop in your own matches means something different in a month when the whole market is down.

## Act Now

The most time-sensitive items, pulled to the top of the page so they don't get buried.

- **Response windows** — how much time is left on the solicitations you're matched to, grouped into buckets. The shortest bucket is where you'll lose opportunities by default if you don't act.
- **Match strength split** — how your current matches divide between hard and soft matches. See [How Solicitation Matching Works](/help/solicitation-matching) for what those mean.
- **Amendment alerts** — solicitations that changed *after* they matched your profiles. An amendment can move a close date, change quantities, or revise requirements, so anything listed here is worth re-reading before you quote.
- **Buy signals** — parts you supply where DLA's own data shows a backorder or a stock level below the reorder point. These are the strongest near-term indicators that a purchase is coming; the underlying data is explained in [Reading Demand & Stock Data](/help/demand-intelligence).

## Your Business

Your own procurement record, drawn from awards tied to your CAGE code.

- **Historical Contract Value** — your lifetime procurement total with the government.
- **Open Matched Solicitations** — currently live solicitations on parts you manufacture or supply.
- **Competitors on Your Parts** — how many distinct vendors have won awards on the same parts as you.
- **Winning price benchmarks** — what winning bids have actually gone for on your parts, so you can price against evidence instead of instinct.
- **Competitor leaderboard** — which vendors win most often on your parts, and how much they've taken.
- **Set-aside win rate** — how you perform in each set-aside category. A high win rate in one category and a low one in another tells you where to concentrate.

## Opportunity Targeting

Forward-looking: where the next contract is likely to come from.

- **Hot parts** — parts in your catalog with the most current activity.
- **Bookings trend** and **Awards over time** — your own award history over time. A flat or falling line against a rising Market Pulse means you're losing share, not that the market shrank.
- **Top awarded parts** — the parts that account for most of your award value.
- **Upcoming solicitations** — what's closing soonest on parts you supply.
- **Market prioritization** — parts *outside* your current catalog, in categories where you already have a track record, that DLA is flagging for a near-term buy — ranked by estimated value. This is the prospecting list: parts worth getting qualified on because you're already credible in that space.

## Bid-Matching Health

How well your [bid-matching profiles](/help/bid-matching-profiles) are actually working.

- **Active profiles** and **Total matches** — how much your profiles are producing.
- **Match trend** — match volume over time. A sudden drop usually means a profile is too narrow, not that the market went quiet — cross-check against Market Pulse.
- **Condition types** — which conditions across your profiles are doing the work.
- **Profile health** — profiles producing nothing, or producing far too much. Both are worth tuning; see [Bid-Matching Recipes and Tuning](/help/bid-matching-recipes).
- **Recent matches** — the latest solicitations matched to your profiles.

## How fresh the numbers are

Market Pulse is shared across all customers and refreshes every few minutes. Your business figures refresh about once a minute. The DLA demand and stock data behind **Buy signals** is published monthly, so it carries an "as of" date and lags reality by a few weeks — see [Important caveats](/help/demand-intelligence#important-caveats) before acting on it.

Every figure is derived from published government data. Forecasts and buy signals raise or lower the *probability* of a solicitation; none of them guarantee one.
